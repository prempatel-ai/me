What a Researcher Does?

Oct 14, 2026 · 2 min read

The plant by the window that kept dying. I am a student, so I haven't earned the word yet. I know what I want from it, though. A researcher picks a question nobody has answered and keeps at it. Most of the time it stays unanswered. They keep at it anyway.

My room has two windows. A plant on the left one is fine. The same plant on the right one keeps dying. I could have bought another plant. I wanted to know why instead.

I wrote my first guess on a sticky note and stuck it on the glass. It said "sun." I swapped the pots, and the left plant was still fine, so I crossed the note out. The second note said "water." I measured it, and the amount was the same. Crossed out. I crossed them out with more pleasure than I'd admit. The glass looked emptier each time, and the answer looked closer.

Then I pushed my hand into the soil by the right window. It was warm. A heater sits right under that window, and the pot was drying from the bottom. Nobody asked me to find that. I couldn't leave it.

That's what I loved. Two weeks of not knowing, then my hand in warm soil, and I knew. Nobody handed it to me, and I could show anyone how I got there.

What Makes a Great Researcher?

So what makes a great researcher? Someone who knows everything? I don't think so. Nobody does, and the person who believes they do has stopped asking. Knowing where to look is better, because it saves time. I never knew about the heater. I only knew to check the room.

Not quitting is better again. The answer usually sits behind boring days when nothing happens, and that's where most people walk away. But I'd trust the honest one most. If you bend a result to match what you hoped for, you haven't found anything. The researcher I want to be can write down the answer they didn't want and keep looking at it.

That means staying in "I don't know" for a long time without lying to yourself. I think that's the real work. Everything else helps you survive it.

Beyond One Field

I don't want to be a researcher of one field. I knew nothing about soil or heat when I started, and I learned what I needed to find the heater. I want to do that with every question I meet. Whatever domain it lives in, I'll go in deep and learn it.

Right now my question is in AI. How can I make a machine smarter? I don't have an answer yet. I have the question.

"From today I want to be that."

This morning I moved the pot an inch to the left and stuck a new note on the glass. It says "day one."
