Learning to Draw Again With AI

Feb 8, 2026

An essay on divergence, taste, and visual exploration in AI-native design workflows by Surya, Nikolai and Sai.

Watch the video introduction here | Try the experimental skill here

The Funnel

CLI-based AI tooling serves as an idea funnel. Little does it matter how broad your research is or how many creative kernels you approach a project with. The second you reduce a vision down to a text prompt, you are forced to make premature choices.

Text prompts act as narrow bottlenecks through which wide design ideas must pass. You cannot easily sketch a spatial feeling, a rhythmic pacing, or an ambiguous mood through words alone without collapsing the very ambiguity that makes early exploration fruitful.

The Image that Understood Itself

Images don't just depict ideas; they understand themselves. When an AI model processes visual representations, it sees structural relationships, composition, tension, and whitespace in ways that language tokens struggle to encode.

A shift towards images is a shift closer to the source material. By working visually with model feedback loops, designers and engineers can preserve divergence rather than immediately snapping to the nearest convergent template.

Paper, Layers, Canvas

Software design originated with physical surfaces: paper, pencils, layout sheets, and drafting tables. Digital canvases gave us layers, infinite boards, and precision vectors. But the latest wave of text-first generation tools momentarily pulled us backwards into linear command prompts.

By restoring visual canvases to AI interfaces, we reclaim the intuitive workspace where ideas collide, mutate, and evolve before code is ever minted.

Taste is Selection

When generation costs approach zero, the scarce commodity is no longer output volume; it is taste. Taste is knowing which variation carries weight, which subtlety should be amplified, and which 99 options must be discarded.

Learning to Draw Again

AI has drawn us back into the terminal, into the heart of the computer. Text is king. But by sticking to this paradigm we risk overlooking other abilities that have recently ballooned in capability, namely images.

And ultimately, this is a shift back to the origins of design in software — by betting on images we are once again picking up the pencil, and setting a sheet of paper in front of us. We just need to learn to draw.
