Policy on the AI Exponential

AI is advancing at exponential speed, and the policymaking process was built for a slower world.

We are sharing two policy proposals to prepare for AI progress. The first, our Advanced AI Framework, offers a roadmap for governing increasingly capable systems, from transparency and independent evaluation to government authority to block or deter dangerous deployments. The second, our Economic Policy Framework, turns to the question of how to prepare workers and the economy for AI's impact and ensure the financial benefits of AI are broadly shared. Together they cover two sides of the same challenge: steering the technology responsibly as it advances, and preparing society for what it brings.

Anthropic's Advanced AI Framework

Read the full Anthropic AI Safety Framework proposal →

We are publishing our proposal for how governments should address catastrophic risks from the most powerful AI models, including granting them the legal authority to block or deter dangerous deployments.

AI capabilities are on a sharp upward trend. A few years ago, AI models could barely write code. This year, Claude Mythos Preview discovered thousands of high-severity zero-day vulnerabilities in critical open-source repositories and infrastructure systems. Within a few more years, models may be able to autonomously orchestrate end-to-end biological or cyber weapons, conduct automated offensive campaigns, or self-replicate across network clusters.

This calls for decisive action and tailored regulation—regulations that are carefully designed to prevent government overreach and protect innovation while enforcing strict boundaries. When a model crosses thresholds where misuse could result in catastrophic harms, voluntary commitments alone are insufficient.

"These rules should apply only to models trained using more than 1025 floating-point operations (FLOPs), developed by organizations earning substantial AI-related revenue. We must protect open-source researchers while holding frontier developers accountable."

Requirements for Frontier Developers

Transparency.

Frontier developers should rigorously test their models across biological, cyber, and CBRN vectors and publish standardized summaries of evaluation results, explaining risk thresholds and red-teaming methodologies.

Independent Evaluation.

Developers must engage at least one qualified third-party auditor or safety evaluation institute (such as the US and UK AISI) to independently verify frontier capability assessments prior to deployment.

Deterministic Gating & Sandboxing.

Probabilistic LLM token outputs must never be granted direct, unmitigated execution authority over financial rails, critical databases, or live infrastructure. Autonomous agent systems must enforce separate, deterministic policy engines where rules, limits, and human-in-the-loop approvals are cryptographically and deterministically gated.

Security & Weights Protection.

Model weights and cluster training environments represent national security assets. Frontier developers must secure infrastructure against sophisticated state-sponsored actors through hardened access controls, confidential computing, and compartmentalized network perimeters.

Enforcement with Teeth.

Governments must retain the explicit legal mechanism to pause, restrict, or revoke deployment licenses when an AI system poses uncontrollable or severe systemic threats to public safety.

Engineering Perspective: Enforcing Deterministic Gating

In my work building AgentPay for the Razorpay AI Buildathon, I implemented this exact separation of concerns: the LLM agent proposes purchases based on user intent, but a deterministic Python policy engine verifies constraints (spend velocity, recipient whitelists, HMAC signatures) before any transaction packet reaches Razorpay's API:

python · agentpay_policy_gate.py 
from pydantic import BaseModel, Field
from typing import Optional
import hmac, hashlib

class TransactionIntent(BaseModel):
    agent_id: str
    amount_inr: float = Field(..., gt=0)
    merchant_vpa: str
    category: str
    reasoning_trace: str

class DeterministicPolicyEngine:
    """Hard boundary: The LLM agent can NEVER execute payments directly."""
    def __init__(self, max_single_limit: float = 5000.0, daily_cap: float = 20000.0):
        self.max_single = max_single_limit
        self.daily_cap = daily_cap

    def evaluate_and_gate(self, intent: TransactionIntent, daily_spent: float) -> dict:
        # Check 1: Absolute hard spending ceiling
        if intent.amount_inr > self.max_single:
            return {"status": "BLOCKED", "code": "EXCEEDS_SINGLE_TRANSACTION_LIMIT"}

        # Check 2: Rolling daily aggregate check
        if (daily_spent + intent.amount_inr) > self.daily_cap:
            return {"status": "REQUIRES_HUMAN_APPROVAL", "code": "DAILY_VELOCITY_CAP_TRIGGERED"}

        # Check 3: Cryptographic idempotency token generation
        token = hmac.new(b"secret_key", f"{intent.agent_id}:{intent.amount_inr}".encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
        return {"status": "APPROVED_FOR_RAZORPAY", "gate_token": token}

Economic Policy & Societal Resilience

In the second half of the framework, Anthropic emphasizes the urgent need for governments to build societal resilience against biological, cyber, and economic shifts:

Biosurveillance & Gene Screening.

Universal screening of gene synthesis orders to prevent dangerous pathogen creation, combined with nationwide wastewater and clinical metagenomic sequencing for early outbreak detection.

Software Hardening on Internet Core.

Deploying automated AI defensive auditing tools to patch legacy memory-unsafe infrastructure (C/C++ codebases in utility grids, air traffic control, and banking rails) before offensive AI exploit agents can weaponize them.

Broadly Shared Economic Upside.

Structuring tax, retraining, and capital distribution frameworks so productivity dividends from automated labor uplift workers rather than concentrating wealth exclusively among compute holders.
